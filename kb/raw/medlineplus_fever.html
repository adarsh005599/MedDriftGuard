Fever

Home > Health Topics

Overview

A fever is a temporary increase in your body temperature, often due to an illness. Having a fever is a sign that something out of the ordinary is going on in your body. For an adult, a fever may be uncomfortable, but usually it isn't a cause for concern unless it reaches 103 F (39.4 C) or higher. For infants and toddlers, a slightly elevated temperature may indicate a serious infection.

Fevers generally go away within a few days. A number of over-the-counter medications lower a fever, but sometimes it is better left untreated because a fever seems to play a key role in helping your body fight off a number of infections.

Common Symptoms

You have a fever when your temperature rises above its normal range. What is normal for you may be a little higher or lower than the average normal temperature of 98.6 F (37 C). Depending on what is causing your fever, additional fever signs and symptoms may include sweating, chills and shivering, headache, muscle aches, loss of appetite, irritability, and general weakness.

Dehydration is also common during fevers because the body loses fluids through sweat and increased respiration. Young children between the ages of 6 months and 5 years might experience febrile seizures during sudden high fevers.

Home Care and Hydration

In most cases, drinking plenty of fluids is the most important self-care measure. Water, clear broths, and oral rehydration solutions help prevent dehydration. Resting is vital because physical activity can raise your body temperature. Staying in a comfortable, cool room and wearing lightweight clothing helps your body dissipate heat naturally.

Do not bundle up in heavy blankets even if you experience chills, as this can trap body heat and push your internal temperature higher. Tepid sponging with lukewarm water can provide comfort, but cold baths or alcohol rubs should never be used.

When to See a Doctor

Seek immediate medical attention if a fever is accompanied by a severe headache, stiff neck, shortness of breath, chest pain, confusion, persistent vomiting, or a sudden unexplained rash. For infants under 3 months old, any rectal temperature of 100.4 F (38 C) or higher requires immediate emergency evaluation by a pediatrician, even if the infant exhibits no other signs of illness.

For adults, consult a healthcare provider if a fever lasts longer than three consecutive days or repeatedly spikes above 103 F despite home measures.
