Overview

A cough is your body's way of clearing foreign substances and mucus from your lungs and upper airway passages. Coughs can either be productive, meaning they bring up phlegm or mucus, or non-productive (dry), producing no secretions. Most acute coughs are caused by viral respiratory infections such as the common cold, acute bronchitis, or influenza.

Chronic coughs, defined as coughs that last longer than eight weeks in adults or four weeks in children, are frequently caused by asthma, postnasal drip from allergic rhinitis, gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD), or environmental irritants such as tobacco smoke.

Home Management and Relief

Staying well-hydrated helps thin secretions in your throat and airways, making it easier to cough up phlegm. Drinking warm fluids such as herbal teas, warm lemon water with honey, or clear broths can soothe irritation in the throat. (Note: honey should never be given to infants under one year of age due to the risk of infant botulism).

Using a cool-mist humidifier or inhaling steam from a warm shower can moisten dry airways and relieve nighttime coughing fits. Elevating your head with extra pillows while sleeping can prevent postnasal drip from triggering throat spasms.

When to See a Doctor

You should seek emergency medical care immediately if you experience shortness of breath, wheezing, coughing up pink frothy phlegm or bright red blood, severe chest tightness, or a bluish discoloration of the lips or face. Consult a doctor if an unexplained cough persists for more than three weeks, is accompanied by unexplained weight loss or night sweats, or makes speaking in full sentences difficult.
