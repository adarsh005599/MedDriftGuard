Overview

A headache is pain or discomfort in the head, scalp, or neck. Most headaches are primary headaches, meaning they are not a symptom of an underlying disease. The most common primary headaches include tension-type headaches, migraines, and cluster headaches. Tension headaches produce dull, aching head pain often described as a tight band around the forehead. Migraines typically involve pulsing or throbbing pain, usually on one side of the head, and are frequently accompanied by sensitivity to light and sound, nausea, or visual auras.

Home Management and Prevention

Many tension headaches improve with relaxation techniques, stress reduction, adequate sleep, and maintaining regular meal schedules to prevent blood sugar drops. Applying a cold cloth or ice pack wrapped in a towel to the forehead or temples can help numb migraine pain, while gentle heat on the neck can relax tight muscles in tension headaches. Resting quietly in a dark, quiet room during an episode is often beneficial.

When to See a Doctor

Seek urgent emergency care if you experience a sudden, explosive headache (often described as the worst headache of your life, known as a thunderclap headache), a headache accompanied by fever, stiff neck, confusion, seizure, double vision, numbness, or difficulty speaking. You should also seek urgent evaluation if a headache follows a recent head injury, or if a new headache pattern develops in someone over age 50.
