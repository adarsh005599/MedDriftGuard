Overview

Hypertension, also known as high blood pressure, is a common condition in which the blood vessels have persistently raised pressure. Blood pressure is written as two numbers: systolic (pressure when the heart beats) and diastolic (pressure when the heart rests between beats). Hypertension is diagnosed if, when measured on two different days, the systolic blood pressure reading on both days is 140 mmHg or greater and the diastolic reading is 90 mmHg or greater.

Hypertension is commonly referred to as a silent killer because most people who have high blood pressure show no warning signs or symptoms until serious organ damage has already occurred.

Symptoms and Complications

Most individuals with high blood pressure do not feel sick. However, very high blood pressure can cause early morning headaches, nosebleeds, irregular heart rhythms, vision changes, and buzzing in the ears (tinnitus). Severe hypertension may cause fatigue, nausea, vomiting, confusion, anxiety, chest pain, and muscle tremors.

If left unmanaged, hypertension damages arterial walls and can lead to severe cardiovascular events such as heart attacks, heart failure, strokes, kidney damage, and vascular dementia.

Lifestyle Modifications

Lifestyle changes play a foundational role in preventing and managing high blood pressure. Key evidence-based modifications include reducing dietary sodium intake to less than 2 grams per day (equivalent to under 5 grams of salt), eating more fresh vegetables and fruits, engaging in regular physical activity such as brisk walking for at least 30 minutes five days a week, avoiding tobacco use, moderating alcohol consumption, and effectively managing chronic mental stress.

When to Seek Emergency Care

A hypertensive crisis occurs when blood pressure spikes to 180/120 mmHg or higher. If a high blood pressure reading is accompanied by chest pain, shortness of breath, back pain, numbness, weakness, change in vision, or difficulty speaking, emergency medical services (such as 112 or 108) must be contacted immediately. Do not attempt to manage a hypertensive crisis at home.
