Overview

Diarrhoea is defined as the passage of three or more loose or liquid stools per day, or more frequently than is normal for the individual. Frequent passing of formed stools is not diarrhoea. Diarrhoea is typically a symptom of an infection in the intestinal tract, which can be caused by a variety of bacterial, viral, and parasitic organisms. The most critical threat posed by acute diarrhoea is dehydration, which occurs when water and essential electrolytes (sodium, potassium, chloride, and bicarbonate) are lost through liquid stools, vomit, sweat, and urine.

Recognizing Dehydration

Mild to moderate dehydration presents with dry mouth and tongue, sunken eyes, decreased skin elasticity (pinched skin goes back slowly), intense thirst, reduced urine output, and dark-colored urine. In severe dehydration, symptoms progress to extreme lethargy or unconsciousness, very low blood pressure, rapid and weak pulse, cool and moist extremities, and inability to drink fluids. Severe dehydration is life-threatening, especially in infants, young children, and elderly individuals.

Oral Rehydration Therapy

The cornerstone of diarrhoea management is immediate oral rehydration therapy using standard Oral Rehydration Salt (ORS) solution mixed in safe drinking water. In home settings where commercial ORS packets are temporarily unavailable, homemade sugar-salt solution (6 level teaspoons of sugar and half level teaspoon of salt dissolved in 1 litre of clean boiled water) or clean rice water, coconut water, and diluted buttermilk can be administered continuously after each loose stool.

Nutritious, easily digestible foods like khichdi, bananas, and curd should continue to be offered. Do not withhold food during diarrhoeal illness.

When to See a Doctor

Seek urgent hospital care if loose stools contain visible blood, if vomiting is continuous preventing fluid retention, if high fever develops, if diarrhoea lasts longer than 48 hours without improvement, or if the individual exhibits signs of severe dehydration such as confusion, unresponsiveness, or absence of urination for over 8 hours.
