Overview

Dengue is a viral infection transmitted to humans through the bite of infected Aedes mosquitoes, primarily Aedes aegypti. The virus is widespread throughout the tropics, with local variations in risk influenced by rainfall, temperature, relative humidity, and rapid unplanned urbanization. While most people who get dengue experience mild or no symptoms, in some individuals the virus can cause severe dengue, a potentially lethal complication that requires urgent hospital management.

Symptoms and Warning Signs

Mild dengue manifests with a sudden onset of high fever (40 C / 104 F) accompanied by at least two of the following: severe headache, pain behind the eyes (retro-orbital pain), muscle and joint aches (sometimes called breakbone fever), nausea, vomiting, swollen glands, and rash. Symptoms usually appear 4 to 10 days after infection and last for 2 to 7 days.

Severe dengue warning signs typically emerge 3 to 7 days after illness onset, often around the time the fever drops below 38 C. Warning signs include severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, rapid breathing, mucosal bleeding (bleeding gums or nose), fluid accumulation, fatigue, restlessness, and blood in vomit or stool. Severe dengue is a medical emergency due to plasma leakage, fluid accumulation, respiratory distress, and organ impairment.

Care and Prevention

There is no specific antiviral treatment for dengue fever. Supportive home care focuses on plenty of bed rest and aggressive oral rehydration with clean water, oral rehydration salts (ORS), and fruit juices to replace electrolytes lost to fever and vomiting. Avoiding mosquito bites during illness is crucial to prevent transmitting the virus to household contacts. Use mosquito nets, window screens, and wear clothing that minimizes skin exposure.

When to Seek Emergency Care

If you or someone in your care displays any warning signs of severe dengue—such as severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, mucosal bleeding, difficulty breathing, or severe lethargy—go to the nearest hospital emergency department immediately. Prompt medical assessment and intravenous fluid administration by trained healthcare professionals reduce dengue mortality rates to below 1 percent.
