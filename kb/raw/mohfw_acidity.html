Overview

Acidity, clinically known as acid reflux or gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD), occurs when stomach acid frequently flows back into the tube connecting your mouth and stomach (esophagus). This backwash of acid can irritate the delicate lining of your esophagus. While occasional acid reflux is very common, chronic reflux occurring more than twice a week can lead to complications such as esophageal inflammation or strictures.

Common Symptoms

A burning sensation in your chest (heartburn), usually occurring after eating and potentially worse at night or while lying down, is the hallmark symptom. Other symptoms include regurgitation of sour or bitter-tasting liquid into the throat, upper abdominal discomfort, difficulty swallowing, a sensation of a lump in your throat, and chronic dry cough or hoarseness in the morning.

Dietary and Lifestyle Measures

Lifestyle adjustments are the first line of management for mild acidity. Helpful practices include eating smaller, more frequent meals rather than large heavy dinners; avoiding lying down for at least 2 to 3 hours after eating; elevating the head of your bed by 6 inches; avoiding tight clothing that puts pressure on the abdomen; maintaining a healthy weight; and moderating consumption of known trigger foods such as deeply fried foods, oily gravies, excess caffeine, carbonated drinks, citrus fruits, and spicy dishes.

Distinguishing from Cardiac Emergencies

It is vital to distinguish between harmless heartburn and serious cardiac events. If chest pain radiates to your left arm, shoulder, jaw, neck, or back, or is accompanied by shortness of breath, cold sweat, lightheadedness, or sudden nausea, treat it immediately as a potential heart emergency and call 112 / 108. Never assume chest pain with radiating discomfort is merely acidity.
